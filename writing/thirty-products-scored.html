Thirty developer products scored 44/100

11 November 2026

I ran the scorecard across thirty products launched on Hacker News — readable public pages only, five more excluded because they were JavaScript-rendered and could not be scored honestly.

Median: 44 out of 100. Twenty-one of thirty landed in the "failing" band, one in "strong". The sample is a specific population, not the market, and the weights are opinionated. But the shape of the failures was consistent enough to be useful.

What was missing most often

  • No content section in the sitemap (19 of 30) — no page capturing demand before someone searches the brand.
  • No discoverable signup path (18 of 30) — the visitor wants to start and there is no obvious route.
  • No free trial or no-card offer (17 of 30) — the only way to try is to pay.
  • No analytics visible (16 of 30) — so no channel can be judged, including the ones that cost money.
  • No price on the landing page (14 of 30) — the buyer assumes a sales call and leaves.

Only seven of thirty had any agent-commerce profile even though assistants are becoming a discovery surface. The variance that surprised me most: products with strong content often had no price on the page at all. They had earned attention and then made the decision step invisible.

How to read this

A low score does not mean a product cannot sell; a high score does not mean it will. It means the public path has a hole. Score one product, fix the two biggest holes, re-score in thirty days. If the score moves and nothing else does, the problem is the offer, not the page.

The tool, method and limitations: shipreadyscore.com/index.
