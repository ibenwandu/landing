The Merchant Center trap: eleven products, zero barcodes

11 November 2026

For months, a small Shopify store had eighty-three listings in Google Merchant Center, all marked Limited, and zero clicks. The products existed. They were in stock. The storefront looked fine. The cause was one empty field, repeated eleven times.

Google marks branded products as Limited when the feed lacks unique product identifiers: a GTIN/MPN pair, or a brand with enough information to trust the identity. The store had eleven products — rope leashes, knitwear, candles — and none carried a barcode. The SKU was a generated Shopify id, not a manufacturer part number. So no product could be matched, and no free listing was served.

The fix is administrative, not creative

For each product: set the brand, add the barcode where the supplier has one, use the supplier's SKU as the MPN, and for genuinely handmade items declare identifier_exists=false instead of inventing an identifier. Then reconcile the feed count with the catalogue (83 feed items, 11 live products — a stale feed is its own trap) and request a re-review.

None of that is marketing. It is switch-flipping. But it is the difference between a store that can be discovered and one that cannot, and it is invisible unless someone looks at the feed instead of the design.

The broader lesson: distribution problems are often data problems wearing a design costume. Before writing a single post or pin, check whether the platform can see the product at all.
